{{ define "content" }} {{/* Sent when a second way of signing in is linked to somebody's account - see specs/account-linking-recovery.md and service/link.go. **This is the only thing that tells the account's owner it happened.** Everything else in that design makes the grant hard to obtain: the re-authentication, the nonce, the single-use token. None of it lets the owner *find out*. A successful trick, or an honest mistake, adds a permanent new way into an account and otherwise says nothing to anybody outside that one browser session. **It carries no grant, and that is what makes it safe to send.** No link, no token, nothing to click and nothing to act on - so phishing a copy of it achieves precisely nothing. That is the difference between this and the emailed confirmation link the spec rejects: that one *was* the authorisation, wearing an email's clothes. It names the provider rather than the address or the Auth0 subject. The subject is a stable identifier for a person at their identity provider and ADR-0008 §1 keeps those out of anything user-facing; the address belongs to the *other* account, and repeating it here would tell whoever reads this email something they may not already know. {{ .Data.Provider }} can legitimately be empty, for a connection service.ProviderName has no name for. The sentence is written so it still reads correctly - "a new way of signing in" - rather than leaving a hole. {{ .Data.When }} is UTC with the zone named, and deliberately not localised: see the field's comment. The time is the part a recipient actually reasons with - "was I signing in at half past three?" is how somebody decides whether this was them - so a *wrong* local time would be worse than an honest UTC one. Transactional, and pointedly not unsubscribable: ADR-0010's unsubscribe covers the onboarding sequence, and somebody who opted out of that has said nothing about whether they want to be told their account has a new key. */}}
